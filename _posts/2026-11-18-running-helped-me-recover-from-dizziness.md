---
title: "Running helped me recover from dizziness"
date: 2026-11-18
layout: post
---

For the past year, I've been suffering from severe migraines and dizziness. Around this time last year, I had a severe episode of dizziness that landed me in the hospital, followed by months of trying to recover from constant dizziness and migraines.

I got multiple diagnoses, but none with any concrete medical evidence to back them up. Ménière's disease, vestibular migraine, burnout, psychological factors, or simply "nothing serious, just relax". :D

But at the same time, nobody gave me any meaningful advice on what to actually do or how to recover. Doctors would usually suggest more sleep (how can anyone sleep properly with two kids, one of them a newborn?), vitamins, anti-migraine medication, or walking. I tried all of them, but nothing seemed to make much of a difference.

I spent almost 4 months dealing with daily dizziness, to the point where I could barely work or even function properly. Walking and certain vestibular exercises would occasionally help, but never enough to make a lasting difference.

And then came the era of LLMs, which made things even worse.

My eyes were hurting from constantly staring at walls of text. I'd close my laptop at the end of the day feeling dizzy, exhausted, and sometimes with a terrible migraine. I tried different glasses, changing display settings, tweaking the various LLM harnesses, even creating special skills to reduce the amount of text being generated. Nothing really helped. The growing volume of PRs, the constant context switching, and the pressure to keep up with everything didn't help either.

And then, one day, I decided to start running.

I was never a runner. I've always exercised, but running was something I actively avoided. I always said I didn't like it, that I got bored easily, and that running alone was just too difficult to enjoy.

The first few runs were exhausting. I could barely finish a lap around the track, then 1 km, then 2 km. Every time, I was close to giving up. But I noticed something interesting: whenever I came back home, I felt better and the dizziness would almost disappear for a few hours.

So I kept going.

Eventually, 2 km became 5, and then 10. And the more I ran, the longer that feeling of relief lasted. First a few hours, then an entire day, sometimes even several days.

Now, if I go a few days without running, I occasionally start feeling a little dizzy again. But whenever I get out and run, the dizziness and migraines seem to go away.

Perhaps it was psychological after all. Perhaps running helps me relax, clear my head, or deal with stress in a way that nothing else managed to. I honestly don't know, but it works for me.

Running alone is still tough, and I still get bored sometimes. But it's also the one thing that has helped me get back to functioning almost 100% and, more importantly, enjoying life again.

Especially now, with LLMs becoming such a big part of my daily work, going for a morning run every couple of days has become almost essential. It helps me reset, gives me energy, and makes it possible to spend the rest of the day in front of a screen without feeling completely drained.

I wish someone had suggested trying this a year ago. It's not some magical cure for dizziness or migraines, but I feel that sometimes the thing that makes the biggest difference is something you never thought would work for you.

And for me, surprisingly, that thing was running.
